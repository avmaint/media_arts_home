Driving the Portman P2 EVO — filament and glow, six times over

A working reference for the P2 EVO in Mode IV "Standard Fine," 69 channels — the fine mode that keeps a real intensity for the glow layer. A fixture with no pan, no optics: just six pixels, each pairing a dim-to-warm tungsten source with an RGBW glow, plus a dual strobe and a bank of built-in macros.

00

What you're actually patching

The P2 EVO is a decorative bar of six pixels. It doesn't move and has no gobos or zoom — its whole vocabulary is two light sources per pixel and how they behave when you dim them.

Two sources in every pixel
  • Main Source (HaloLED) — a warm tungsten-emulation filament, the visible "bulb." One intensity per pixel; there are six.
  • Glow (RGBW) — a saturated colour wash sitting behind each filament. Red / Green / Blue / White per pixel, six of them.
  • They're independent: a warm filament in front, any colour glow behind. Layering the two is the entire aesthetic of the fixture.
Dim-to-warm is automatic
  • The Main Source emulates halogen: as you dim it, it warms — sliding down toward ~1600 K like a real tungsten lamp cooling, up to ~3200 K at full.
  • You don't set colour temperature on a channel — you set intensity, and the fixture's dimming curve (chosen on the Control channel) decides how warm it gets. That's why there's no CCT channel in the map.
How it lands in grandMA3
  • Six pixels, each carrying a Dimmer (the warm source) plus RGBW (the glow), patch as subfixtures. In Standard Fine, every one of those is 16-bit.
  • Standard Fine adds one attribute the pixel modes don't have: a 16-bit glow master dimmer (ch 13–14) sitting above all six RGBW cells. It's the reason to be in this mode — see the Dimmer group below.
  • Colour and intensity chases run through subfixture selection + MAtricks across the six pixels — the same pixel workflow as the Tornado's rings, just six cells instead of forty.
Verify on your profile

Whether the warm source and the glow import as one subfixture per pixel or two, and the exact attribute spellings, depend on the GDTF/library revision. Patch one, expand it in the fixture sheet, and read the subfixture list before scripting.

Coming from Pixel Fine

The glow master is a new attribute, not a renamed one. Cues recorded while the fixture was in a pixel mode have nothing stored for it, so they'll play back with the glow at its default — quite possibly dark — until you set and store a level. Check one cue on a bench unit before changing the mode on the whole rig. The fixture end is MY PERSONAL SETTINGS → DMX MODE → STANDARD FINE, or over RDM from the console; then repatch the mode in MA3 and re-address (69 ch apiece).

Where a control actually lives

The encoder bar shows up to five dual encoders at a time. A feature group with more than that spreads across several encoder pages, one per feature — the Encoder Page button to the left of the encoders names the current feature and shows how many pages the group has. Tap it to cycle, or tap and swipe for the list. Wherever a control on this fixture is not on the first page of its group, this document flags it later page.

02

Feature group by feature group

In Standard Fine the fixture only really uses four groups: Dimmer (six warm sources plus the glow master), Colour (six RGBW glows + a virtual wheel), Beam (two strobes), and Control (the macro engine plus two fixture settings). No Position, Gobo, Focus, or Shapers exist.

Dimmer

Preset Type 1

The heart of this fixture — six warm Main-Source filaments, the glow master, and the macro master.

What's here
  • Dimmer per pixel (ch 1–12) — the HaloLED warm source, 16-bit. At 50, Full, Off as usual. Dimming it triggers the dim-to-warm shift; the curve is set on Control (§ below).
  • Intensity Glow RGBW (ch 13–14) later page — one 16-bit master over all six glow cells. Colour lives in the Colour group; level lives here.
  • Intensity Macro (ch 66) later page — master level for the built-in macro engine's output.
Fixture 40.1 Thru 40.6 At 20 — low = deep candle-warm across the bar
Why the glow master matters

grandMA3's intensity machinery — executor faders set to Master, intensity group masters, the grand master — scales the Dimmer attribute and nothing else. In a pixel mode the glow has no dimmer, so pulling a fader takes the filaments down and leaves the RGBW glow sitting at full. Here it dims with everything else. It also means a glow colour preset can stay a pure colour and be recalled at any level, instead of you scaling RGBW values and walking the hue on the way down.

P2 EVO · CH 1–12 · 13–14 There's no colour-temperature channel for the warm source — warmth is purely a function of dim level and the chosen curve. To hold a colour temperature you hold a dim level. The glow master is one master for all six cells, not one per pixel: per-pixel glow chases still come from the RGBW values themselves.

Color

Preset Type 4

The glow layer — six RGBW cells behind the filaments, plus a quick virtual colour wheel.

Per-pixel RGBW glow
  • Red Green Blue White per pixel (ch 15–62), 16-bit each. Use the Colour Picker per pixel; the dedicated White emitter is there for pastels and tints the RGB can't reach cleanly.
  • Mix at full and let the glow master carry the level — you'll get cleaner colour presets and cleaner fades than scaling the emitters.
  • Select all six and drive them with MAtricks + colour phasers for chases and rainbows along the bar.
Virtual colour wheel — Colour Wheel All Glow (ch 65) later page
  • A slotted attribute that sweeps all glow pixels through one hue ramp: open, then white, then a linear R→G→B→R walk, and a spinning mode up top. The fast way to colour the whole glow without mixing RGBW by hand.
P2 EVO · glow ch 15–62 · wheel ch 65 The glow is additive RGBW — no CMY, no gel wheel. In Standard Fine the glow is laid out pixel by pixel after the master: pixel 1 at 15–22, pixel 2 at 23–30, and so on in eights. The virtual wheel is global (all six glows together); for per-pixel colour, mix RGBW on each pixel instead.

Beam

Preset Type 5

Two independent strobes — one for the filaments, one for the glow.

  • Strobe Main Source (ch 63) — strobes the warm filaments: open, closed, ramp 0.5–30 Hz, and random. Push for the named ranges.
  • Strobe Glow (ch 64) — the same, but on the RGBW glow only.
P2 EVO · CH 63–64 Because the two strobes are separate, you can flash the glow while the filaments hold steady (or the reverse) — a signature effect. Both are global (all six pixels). No prism, gobo, frost, or iris exist on this fixture.

Control

Preset Type 7

The built-in macro engine, plus two settings that live on the Control channel.

  • Macro (ch 67) — selects a built-in macro: 0 off, then 1–3 Macro 1, 4–7 Macro 2, and on in fours. Macro Speed (ch 68) page 2 paces it; Intensity Macro (ch 66) masters its level.
  • Control (ch 69) later page — fixture settings, not looks: 0–30 no function, 31–40 / 41–50 / 51–60 flicker mode 1 / 2 / 3, 61–70 dimming curve linear, 71–80 logarithmic, 246–255 default all values. Hold the value ≥5 seconds to apply.
"Flicker mode" is not a flicker effect

Portman's flicker modes 1/2/3 set the LED driving frequency — 2.4 kHz, 5.1 kHz and 12 kHz — to keep the fixture from beating against a camera shutter. Nothing about them is visible to the eye, so setting one and watching for a candle will show you exactly nothing. For a visible flicker, use a macro (ch 67, with Intensity Macro on ch 66 above zero) or build a dimmer phaser on the console.

P2 EVO · CH 66–69 Keep the Control channel out of playback cues: it only acts after a value is held for five seconds, so a slow crossfade parked on 61–80 can change your dimming curve mid-show. Set it once, then park it.

Position · Gobo · Focus · Shapers

not on this fixture

All four are empty for the P2 EVO — it's a static decorative source with no movement or optics. Its "movement" is entirely dimmer phasers, macros, and glow-colour chases. Tabbing to these banks on the encoder bar will show nothing to control.

03

Programmer housekeeping

Two mechanics that save you from mystery states while building looks — identical to any grandMA3 fixture.

Activate vs. deactivate

Only active values store by default. A value can sit in the programmer inactive — present, affecting output, but skipped by Store/Update. Please Please activates everything for the selection; Off then a tap on a value knocks that one out; Off + a feature-group bank button releases the whole group.

The three states of Clear

Clear ×1 — deselect fixtures (values stay, still active) Clear ×2 — values stay but go deactivated (a store now = empty) Clear ×3 — programmer emptied, values released

Hold Clear for a second to jump straight to a full release.

Warm vs. glow storing

It's easy to build a look with the warm sources active but the glow deactivated (or vice-versa). Before storing, re-select the fixture and Please Please so the Dimmer, the glow master and the RGBW layers all go into the cue together — a glow colour with no master level stored is a cue that plays back dark.

The short version

White on red is yours and will store. Red text on grey is yours but deactivated and won't. Everything else on a grey background is playback telling you where the level came from. On the P2 EVO, the purple phaser layer is where your glow chases live.
