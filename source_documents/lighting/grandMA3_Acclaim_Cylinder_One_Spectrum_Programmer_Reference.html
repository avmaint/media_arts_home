Driving the Acclaim Cylinder One Spectrum — five emitters or one white

A working reference for the Cylinder One Spectrum in 13 Channel (16-bit) mode: five calibrated emitters at 16-bit, a single-channel CCT selector that overrides all of them, and a 16-bit intensity. An architectural downlight with no movement and no optics to program — the whole job is colour and level, and knowing which of the two colour systems is in charge.

00

What you're actually patching

A self-contained downlight built around Acclaim's Spectrum Five engine. It doesn't move, has no gobos, no strobe and no shutter — it mixes colour, picks a white, and dims.

Five emitters, one engine
  • Red · Green · Blue · Amber · Lime — RGBAL, each on its own 16-bit pair. The amber and lime are what let it reach believable whites and skin tones rather than the greenish RGB approximation.
  • Every fixture is ColorSync-calibrated at the factory so that whites match across units. That calibration applies to the CCT selector in this mode — it's the reason to use the CCT channel rather than hand-mixing a white.
  • Output up to 8,610 lumens, 100–277 VAC, IP40 — this is an architectural fixture that happens to speak DMX, not a stage light.
Two colour systems, one at a time
  • Mix — drive the five emitters on channels 1–10 and leave the CCT channel at zero.
  • CCT select — put any non-zero value on channel 11 and the fixture serves a calibrated white from 1800 K to 8000 K, ignoring channels 1–10 entirely.
  • There is no blending between the two. Channel 11 is a hard override, and that's the single thing most likely to bite you in a cue. See the callout in the Colour group below.
How it lands in grandMA3
  • One fixture, no subfixtures — a Dimmer, five colour attributes, and a colour-temperature attribute. Nothing to expand, nothing to MAtrick.
  • Intensity is its own 16-bit attribute, so the console's intensity machinery (executor faders, group masters, grand master) reaches it properly and colour presets stay pure.
  • Attribute naming depends on the GDTF: the CCT selector may appear as CTC, ColorTemperature or a slotted colour-control attribute, and Lime may be Lime or ColorAdd_L. Push an encoder to read the live name before scripting.
Configuration is RDM, not DMX

There is no control channel on this fixture. Personality, DMX address, dimming behaviour, solo mode and internal colour are all set over RDM (Acclaim document the DMXcat-E workflow). Nothing you store in a cue can reconfigure the fixture — and nothing you fat-finger in the programmer can either. Aria X2 wireless DMX is fitted as standard, so check whether a given fixture is listening on the cable or over the air before you chase a dead channel.

What it does when DMX stops

The fixture's Solo setting decides: BO blacks out, Hold freezes the last frame, Internal plays a stored colour. In a house rig that matters — a console reboot with Solo set to Hold leaves the room lit at whatever was on stage. Worth confirming over RDM before you rely on either behaviour.

Where a control actually lives

The encoder bar shows up to five dual encoders at a time. A feature group with more than that spreads across several encoder pages, one per feature — the Encoder Page button to the left of the encoders names the current feature and shows how many pages the group has. Tap it to cycle, or tap and swipe for the list. Wherever a control on this fixture is not on the first page of its group, this document flags it later page.

Selecting and setting

Fixture 60 Thru 65 — the downlight run At 50 — intensity to 50%, 16-bit under the hood At Preset 4.x — recall a colour preset (mix or CCT, not both)

Build two families of colour presets and keep them apart: mixes with CCT at zero, whites with CCT set. A preset that contains both is a preset whose colour half does nothing.

02

Feature group by feature group

Two groups carry everything: Dimmer and Colour. Position, Gobo, Beam, Focus, Control and Shapers are all empty — there is nothing on this fixture to put in them.

Dimmer

Preset Type 1

One 16-bit intensity, master over whichever colour system is running.

  • Dimmer (ch 12–13) — coarse and fine. At 50, Full, Off as usual.
  • It masters both the emitter mix and the CCT white, so you never dim by pulling colour values down.
CYLINDER · CH 12–13 16-bit intensity is worth having on a downlight that lives at low levels — 65,535 steps is the difference between a clean 2% and a visible step. Make sure your fades actually use it: a cue fading 0→3% on an 8-bit profile will stair-step where this one won't.

Color

Preset Type 4

Five emitters at 16-bit, or one calibrated white — never both at once.

Mixing — Red Green Blue Amber Lime (ch 1–10)
  • Five emitters fill one encoder page exactly, so the mix is all on the first page — the CCT selector is not. Each emitter is a coarse/fine pair, 16-bit. The Colour Picker drives R/G/B; amber and lime come in through the additive attributes or a colour preset built by hand.
  • Amber pulls a mix warm without the pink cast RGB-only ambers get; lime fills the gap that makes RGB whites look green. Both matter most at low saturation.
  • Channel 11 must be at zero for any of this to reach the emitters.
White — CCT (ch 11, 8-bit) later page
  • A single 8-bit channel selects a calibrated white: 0 = off (mixing enabled), 1 ≈ 1800 K, 255 = 8000 K, in steps of four or five DMX values per 100 K. The full table is in §05.
  • This is the ColorSync-calibrated path — it's what keeps a row of fixtures matching at 3000 K. Hand-mixed whites will drift between units; CCT whites won't.
  • Only 8-bit, so whites step rather than glide. A slow CCT fade across the range will show it.
The override that will bite you

Any non-zero value on channel 11 makes the fixture ignore channels 1–10 completely. The programmer will happily show you a saturated blue mix that the fixture is not producing, because a CCT value from an earlier cue is still tracking. If a colour won't take, look at CCT first: it needs to be at zero, not merely low. Conversely, storing a mix into a cue without explicitly storing CCT at zero leaves the door open for a tracked white to override it later.

CYLINDER · CH 1–10 · CH 11 Treat CCT as a mode switch rather than a colour attribute. The tidy habit is to put an explicit CCT value — zero or a white — in every colour cue, so the fixture is never running on a value that arrived by tracking.

Position · Gobo · Beam · Focus · Control · Shapers

not on this fixture

All empty. No pan or tilt, no zoom, no strobe or shutter, no macros, no control channel. In 13-channel mode the fixture's entire DMX vocabulary is five emitters, one CCT selector and an intensity — everything else about it is configured over RDM and stays out of the show file.

03

Programmer housekeeping

Two mechanics that save you from mystery states while building looks — identical to any grandMA3 fixture.

Activate vs. deactivate

Only active values store by default. A value can sit in the programmer inactive — present, affecting output, but skipped by Store/Update. Please Please activates everything for the selection; Off then a tap on a value knocks that one out; Off + a feature-group bank button releases the whole group.

The three states of Clear

Clear ×1 — deselect fixtures (values stay, still active) Clear ×2 — values stay but go deactivated (a store now = empty) Clear ×3 — programmer emptied, values released

Hold Clear for a second to jump straight to a full release.

Store the mode, not just the colour

Because CCT overrides the mix, a half-stored colour is worse here than on most fixtures. Re-select and Please Please before storing so the emitters and the CCT channel both land in the cue — including the zero that says "let the mix through."

The short version

White on red is yours and will store. Red text on grey is yours but deactivated and won't. Everything else on a grey background is playback telling you where the level came from. On this fixture, check the CCT cell whenever a colour looks wrong — a tracked magenta value there beats anything you mix.
